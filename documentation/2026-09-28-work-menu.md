# The bar, a tab for each kind of work, and the bodies in detail

- **Date:** 28 September 2026
- **Branch:** `claude/eager-ramanujan-blh51w`
- **Why:** The hero's top row was only the kind filter, a line of mono words. Meet
  asked for a navbar in the spirit of accessgrid.com's (a compact bar whose menus open
  into illustrated panels) that shows off the celestial body standing for each kind of
  work. After a first version with one `work` menu, he asked for a tab per kind,
  panels headed by what each kind is (not the body's name), and bodies detailed enough
  to be recognised on their own: "a nebula should look and feel like a nebula", and a
  black hole should convey its grandness and scale.

## The bar (`src/components/universe/nav.tsx`)

A thin bar at top centre in a hairline frame, on solid black so the stars don't run
through the words. No pill, no blur, no shadow: the Terminal system's "type and
hairlines". From left:

- the mark, and "Meet Bhatt" (back to the top). The mark was the pale blue dot, as a
  point of light; on 29 September Meet had it removed, and its space is held empty
  (`.brandMark`) until he adds a logo;
- a tab per kind, each with its mark in the kind's colour, in the order Meet chose
  (29 September): `o education`, `= experience`, `+ projects`, `× research`,
  `~ leadership`. The order is `KIND_ORDER`, so the phone's chips and the map's key
  follow it. The open tab is underlined in its kind's colour; the kind the map is
  filtered to stays lit;
- `story`, a page; then, after a short hairline, the ways to get in touch: `@ say hello`
  (hidden below 1100 px wide; the `@` is its mark, as each kind has one, so it reads as
  an address, and hovering shows the address) and `resume`, the one hairline-boxed call
  to action. `story` and `say hello` had looked like the same kind of button.

The site-wide link style (`base.css`) gives every anchor a bottom border and turns every
border blue on hover, which put a blue line under `story` and `say hello`. In the bar
and its panels that stands down: links light by colour, and rows keep their hairline.

**Arriving and docking (29 September).** The bar arrives with the hero: as the
opening's lines settle, its frame drops in from above and its items come in after it,
left to right. Past the hero it docks, so the work, not the bar, holds the eye, while
every page stays one click away: it rises to the top edge, its frame widens to the
edges of the screen and slims (40 px to 36 px tall), and its outline dissolves into a
single hairline along the bottom. Its contents do not move: they stay centred at the
width they had (at 1440 wide the name sits at x = 223 before and after), and only the
frame around them changes, on one curve over 620 ms. Scrolling back to the hero
reverses it. A panel opened from the docked strip hangs centred under the contents at
its usual width. (A first version instead folded the bar into a narrow pill of marks,
unfolding on hover; Meet found it didn't look good, and wanted every page named.)

The bar is fixed to the viewport and outside the pinned stage. The stage is a layer
of its own under the chapters' text, so a panel inside it was painted over by the
hero's headline. It rises in with the rest of the hero when the opening lands.

## The panels

Hovering a tab opens its panel after 90 ms. Moving along the tabs with a panel open
morphs it from one kind to the next rather than swapping it (29 September; Meet asked
for a morph, not a fade out and in):

- the body morphs glyph by glyph: in one pass over the grid, each cell shows the old
  body, the new one, or, at the moving edge between them, a scrambled glyph; the edge
  spreads out from the centre with some grain, over 520 ms, while the two bodies' light
  passes from one to the other (`drawBody` carries both palettes, fourteen tiers);
- the words scramble into the next kind's, left to right, each character passing
  through a glyph (`Scramble`), rows kept by position so each piece of work becomes
  the next;
- the panel's height glides to fit, since kinds hold different numbers of pieces. It closes 240 ms after the pointer leaves, with a bridge over
  the gap under the bar, and on Escape, a click elsewhere, or a scroll. Keyboard: Enter
  or Space opens a tab's panel, Left and Right move along the tabs, Down moves into the
  panel. A closed panel is `inert`.

Each panel's top edge is lit in its kind's colour. Left, the body, large and live.
Right:

- "drawn as a nebula" (the key, in mono) and how many pieces of work it holds;
- the heading is the kind: **Professional experience**, **Research**, **Projects**,
  **Leadership**, **Education** (`title` in `encoding.ts`);
- one line on why that body stands for it (`about`);
- the work itself, a hairline row each: name, dates, and a brief (`brief` in
  `src/content/work.ts`) saying what it was about, written for someone reading Meet's
  resume: a project's or a piece of research's aim, or a role and its scope, rather than
  test numbers (29 September; the rows used to show each unit's result, such as a
  model's accuracy). Each links to its `/work/[slug]` page;
- `show on the map`, which filters the map to the kind (and from the hero goes to the
  map), and a link to the index.

Hovering the body brings it up, frames it with the four-tick reticle, and the scanner
follows the pointer across it, showing its hidden structure. Opening a panel, the body
resolves out of a point of light.

On a phone the bar keeps the mark, one `work` tab and `resume`; the panel becomes a
sheet with the kinds as a row of chips over the chosen kind's body and work, and
`story` and `say hello` at its foot.

## The portraits (`src/components/universe/portraits.ts`)

The map's renderers (`bodies.ts`) are tuned to read at a distance and are unchanged.
The panels draw portraits: the same glyphs and colours on a much finer grid (cells
about 3 px wide rather than 7), with light under and over the glyphs, and the
features that make each body recognisable with no context:

- **Star:** the map's own renderer and warm glow, at the glyph size (4.6 px) it had in
  the first version of the menu. A detailed portrait (granulation, sunspots,
  prominences as loops of light, diffraction rays) was tried; Meet preferred the
  original, on 29 September.
- **Nebula:** gas pushed around by itself (domain-warped noise), drawn out into
  filaments; dark dust lanes; an irregular edge; a warm heart; young stars with
  diffraction spikes; a rose glow beneath. Its gas field is costly, so it is worked out
  four times a second and kept.
- **Planet:** a banded giant with a storm, rings with a gap, the rings' shadow across
  the disc and the planet's across the rings, and a small moon.
- **Constellation:** a star chart of the Plough, stars sized by brightness (with Alcor
  beside Mizar), lines stopping short of the stars, a faint coordinate grid, and the
  Milky Way behind.
- **Black hole:** drawn to feel enormous. The accretion disk runs off both edges of the
  frame, streaming in turbulent bands, much brighter on the side turning toward us. Its
  far side is lensed up over the shadow into a full arc and under it into a thin one;
  its near side crosses in front of the shadow. A bright photon ring with a fainter one
  outside it, a wide flat glow, and the starlight behind bent by the hole: each star is
  seen pushed outward and stretched round it into a short arc.

`drawBody` takes an optional renderer and extent, and can leave out its own light, so
the portraits reuse the map's grid-drawing path.

## Verification

`npm run ci` passes. Checked in Chrome at 1440×900 and 390×844: every tab's panel,
hover with reticle and scanner, choosing a kind from the hero, the keyboard, Escape,
scroll to close, and the phone sheet's chips. No console errors. In headless Chrome
(software rendering, with the map still drawing underneath) a frame with a panel open
takes 26 to 40 ms; the nebula is the heaviest.
