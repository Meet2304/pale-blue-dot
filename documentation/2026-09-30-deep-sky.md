# The second chapter: the deep sky, and the void at the end

- **Date:** 30 September 2026
- **Branch:** `claude/eager-ramanujan-blh51w`
- **Why:** Meet didn't like the move from the hero to the map (Earth dropped away
  down the screen and then rose into the galaxy), didn't want the second chapter to
  lay out everything he has done, and didn't want the turning galaxy or the orbiting
  years. He asked for a direct, smooth pull-back to a majestic view of nebulae,
  galaxies and the rest, alive only through ASCII and dither, with a line about his
  impact on the pale blue dot. He left the look to us.

## The story

Meet's words: this is Earth; zoom out, and this is the impact I've had on it so far;
scroll further to dive deeper into that impact; and the last scene is a void around
it, to show there is a long way to go. It has to be plain enough for a recruiter to
follow at a glance.

1. **Earth.** "Hola! I'm Meet. This pale blue dot is where I build things."
   "Scroll to zoom out".
2. **The pull-back.** The camera pulls straight back from Earth. Earth shrinks to a
   point of light where it stands, stars stream past towards it, and the deep sky
   arrives from the edges and settles around the dot.
3. **Impact** ("Zoom out"). **"This is my impact on Earth, so far."**, spoken in as
   it scrolls into view, then "Every light out here is something I've built,
   researched or led." and "Scroll to dive in, year by year". The dot is marked "you
   are here", and a faint ping goes out from it every few seconds so the eye can
   find it.
4. **The years.** The camera dives back in to each year, and that year's work
   resolves out of the dark.
5. **What's next.** The camera flies back out through the sky and on, until all of
   it is one small patch of light (about 280 px across) in the dark: **"There's a
   long way to go."** "Everything I've done so far fits in that one patch of light.
   All the dark around it is still to build." Then the links.

The chapter ticks read Earth, Impact, the years, Next.

### Revised the same day

The first version's line, "All of this, and one pale blue dot. This is my impact on
it, one year at a time.", and its ending, back to the sky a little further out ("All
of it happened here."), didn't tell the story Meet wanted, and he disliked the line.
The sky now _is_ the impact so far, and the ending is the void. For the void the
whole sky fades out towards a soft oval edge (`islandAt` in `cosmos.ts`), so from
far enough away it is one patch of light with nothing around it; the faint stars
drop out first, the spikes shrink, the background stars all but vanish, and "you
are here" goes, leaving the dot and its ping.

## Why the old move swung down and up

The camera interpolated its centre linearly in world space while the zoom changed
exponentially. Early in the move the zoom is still high, so a small step of the
centre is a large step on screen: Earth raced away down the screen, and came back
up as the zoom caught up.

Now each move keeps its subject on a straight line across the screen (`camAt` in
`universe-canvas.tsx`). The subject is whatever the closer of the two framings is
looking at: Earth, for the pull-back, or the year's cluster, for a dive. The camera
is solved around it each frame, with the zoom in log space as before. From the hero
to the sky, Earth's anchor moves from (0.63, 0.5) of the screen to (0.64, 0.52),
so the dot barely moves.

Two more things make it smooth:

- **The camera follows the scroll with easing** (`FOLLOW`, about 90% of the way in
  a quarter of a second), so a mouse wheel's steps become one glide. Not under
  reduced motion.
- **The pull-back starts as soon as the visitor scrolls**, on a smootherstep curve.
  Later moves still hold for a moment, so the text beside them can be read.
- **Shells of stars pass on the way.** Five shells, at scales five times apart, from
  just beyond Earth to the edge of the sky. Each streams in towards the dot and
  fades as it gathers, while the next arrives from the edges, so the move reads as
  travel, not as a crossfade between two pictures. They play on the last move back
  out too.

## The deep sky (`src/components/universe/cosmos.ts`)

It replaces `galaxy.ts`. From left to right on a wide screen:

- cliffs of dust along the bottom, sinking away under the copy on the left, with lit
  amber edges and five pillars rising out of them, and gas streaming up off them;
- a young cluster of hot blue stars above the cliffs, which is what lights them;
- a grand spiral galaxy over the dot, a small spiral far off, a galaxy seen edge on
  with its dust lane, three ellipticals, a ring nebula, a globular cluster;
- a thin cloud of hydrogen in the upper left, and 130 faint, far galaxies, most of
  them reddened;
- eight foreground stars with the six long spikes and two short ones of a segmented
  mirror;
- Earth in a dark pocket of its own, so the dot is never lost in the glow.

Nothing turns and nothing orbits. The life is in the characters:

- every character cell re-decides its glyph and its level on its own clock (1.4 to
  4 s), and dense gas picks among a few glyphs, so it never settles into stripes;
- the gas breathes in patches;
- the glow under the characters is an ordered (Bayer) dither on a 3 px grid, whose
  dots each waver a little at their own moments, so it sparkles;
- the stars twinkle.

**How it is drawn.** The light is measured once into a texture over a fixed patch
of the world (2000 × 1700 Earth radii, 4 per texel), a few rows a frame from the
first frame, so it is ready long before anyone scrolls there (about 2 s). Every
frame, two layers read it through the camera: the dither on a fine screen grid and
the characters on the terminal's grid. Both use the same Bayer matrix to pick
between the four texels around a point, so where two colours meet the edge is
dithered too, rather than stepping along the texture's squares.

**Cost.** The two layers are drawn offscreen and redrawn when the camera moves or
every 80 ms; frames in between copy them. Stars and spikes are drawn every frame.
Measured in Chrome at 1440 × 900 on this machine: 7 ms a frame at rest on the sky
(the hero is 14 ms), and a median of 7 ms (90th percentile 21 ms) while scrolling
through the pull-back.

The framing fills the screen: about 1000 Earth radii across on a wide screen. On a
phone the sky is a wider slice (560 across), with Earth near the top, above the copy.

## The stir: the sky answers the pointer

Meet asked for the page to be alive under the cursor, subtly: the content around
the pointer changes, but the interaction itself is never directly visible.

The pointer is followed with some weight (it lags, then settles). Around where it
rests, within about 130 px:

- the gas gathers a little light, and dim gas nearby grows a few characters;
- its characters re-decide themselves several times faster, so the gas stirs;
- the dither thickens and sparkles more;
- stars, characters and glow are bent outward by up to 7 px, most at 0.7 of the
  radius and not at all at the centre, the way light bends around a mass.

Moving leaves a wake, on a coarse 24 px grid, that stirs and brightens what it
crosses and fades within two seconds. There is no mark at the pointer, no ring and
no outline. The stir arrives and ebbs over a second or so as the pointer enters or
leaves the sky; over the copy it is off. Off under reduced motion.

It runs at full frame rate by redrawing only the patch the stir reaches, every
frame, between the whole redraws every 80 ms; once the pointer is still and the
wake has faded, those patch redraws stop. Measured in Chrome at 1440 × 900: 7 ms a
frame with the pointer resting on the sky, and 7 ms (90th percentile 21 ms) while
it moves, the same as without it.

## What else changed

- The map is gone: its key (which body is which kind of work), its rules, the orbit
  rings around Earth, the year labels and the thread from a hovered body home. The
  work menu in the bar still names each kind's body.
- The years no longer orbit Earth; each cluster sits still where the layout puts it.
- In the bar's panel, "show on the map" is now "show only these", and from the hero
  or the sky it flies on to the first year. The count reads "3 in all".
- The hero's hint, "Scroll to see my work", now says "Scroll to zoom out".
