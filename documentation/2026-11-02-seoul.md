# Seoul: the summit on the award's page, a glyph-orb Earth, the people met, and a faster site

- **Date:** 2 November 2026
- **Branch:** `claude/epic-maxwell-9b4son` (from `main`)
- **Why:** The World Bank's Global AI & Digital Summit in Seoul (19 to 22 October) has
  happened, and Meet presented Vitamin Bob there on 21 October. Meet asked for the
  journey on the hackathon page to have a much larger globe and text, and to cover the
  time in Seoul: the key events, the people met (each name linked to their LinkedIn), and
  the city. Meet also asked for photos that show the achievement, on the page and in the
  award's section on the home page, with animation and hover interactions in the site's
  design language.

  In a second round Meet asked for:
  - the sunset photo to move to the way home (it was taken on the flight back, by way
    of Tokyo);
  - a new photo of the northern lights;
  - every globe and body on the site to look like React Bits' Glyph Orb, polished, with no
    line round its edge and clearly in front of the sky;
  - the people's roles in the days, and the conversation with Matt and Hayley about taking
    Vitamin Bob forward;
  - a music switch that obviously means music, animated.

  In a third round Meet asked for:
  - the album on the home page to open at once when clicked (it was slow), with its arrows
    on its left and right edges rather than under it;
  - fewer, fuller days: "Wheels up" with "Northern lights", "Seoul, before dawn" with "The
    city", and the two wins together, with the LinkedIn posts in that day rather than in
    a section of their own; and "All three tracks" before "Meeting Anna Bjerde";
  - "The people" rebuilt so their faces are clear;
  - one width for every page, with the same space at each side (the journey ran wider than
    the rest of the hackathon page);
  - the content, not the globe, to be what the journey puts first;
  - no vertical line anywhere, in any design;
  - the supernova at the top of the hackathon page to be interactive, and fun;
  - the whole site measured, and made fast to load and quick to respond.

  In a fourth round Meet asked for:
  - the supernova on the hackathon page to be a small side element, never the centre of
    attention, with no words under it, and to go off by itself every ten seconds or so,
    on the home page's award chapter too;
  - the page's index on every section, the journey and the people included;
  - the people side by side, on the same lines, the groups told clearly apart, with no
    empty space;
  - a portrait each for Jonathan (the man) and MJ (the woman), focused on them;
  - the three bullet points under "How it works" removed;
  - every small instruction removed from the site ("Point to move the light · drag to
    turn" under the globe, and the like);
  - the stars in the background twinkling, subtly, on every page.

  In a fifth round Meet asked for the supernova to be much larger again, but faded, part
  of the background rather than something on the page.

  In a sixth round Meet asked for:
  - the supernova to stay on screen, in the background, as the page scrolls;
  - more time between its bursts;
  - a larger globe on the journey, with the days taking even more of the width, a little
    overlap allowed.

  In a seventh round Meet asked for the page's first screen to be only the introduction,
  with "How it works" no longer showing at its foot, and for the page to be made for
  phones.

  including the GPS location one photo carried. Meet's Seoul collage was cut into its five
  photos. They are 2.0 MB in all, and each loads only when it's within two screens.
- **The content** (`work.ts`):

    centre of their face.
  - `people`, with their LinkedIn links.
  - The journey's new days.
  - The intro, the brief and "What I did" now say the talk happened.
  - A `stage` line for the talk.
  - The news line after the turn: "Back from Seoul: on stage at the World Bank's Global
    AI & Digital Summit, after two hackathon wins."
- **The head of the award's page.** The title is now "Two wins, and a stage in Seoul". The
  photo of the hall, with Meet on stage and on both big screens, sits under the intro with
  "Spoke in Seoul, 21 Oct, 10:30".
- **The journey** (`journey.tsx`, `journey.module.css`) moves out of the column and opens to
  almost the full width of the page.
  - The globe is about 1.7× larger, and the titles of the days are about 1.8× larger.
  - The flight is drawn as far as the day being read: the plane is a point of light at
    the head of the flown track.
  - Over the top of the world, the **northern lights** come out round the magnetic pole:

  - The globe is a **glyph orb** (see below), always whole: there is no ring at its edge.
    Once the plane lands it comes a little nearer (×1.14). Pointed at, its **light follows

  - The coastline is sampled bilinearly, so it holds up as a line.
  - Each day in Seoul sends out a **ping** of two rings from the pin.
  - A place is pinned once the story reaches it, except Seoul, which the flight's track
    already points to.
  - The way home is drawn as a leg from Incheon to Tokyo (`TYO`, the city's code, since the
    airport isn't known), flown on the last day.
- **The days** (after the third round):

  - Two wins, 6 and 14 Oct: the World Bank Group's Health track and Hack-Nation's grand

  - Wheels up, and the northern lights: the flight out and Meet's photo of the aurora.

- **The prints** (`prints.tsx`). Each day's photos sit in rows of one height, each in its
  own shape and a little askew.
  - **Developing:** the first time a day is read, its prints develop in one after another.
    Each comes out of a white blur, through grey, into colour, and rises as it settles.
  - **Pointer:** a print straightens, lifts and leans towards the pointer, with a sheen.
  - **Faces:** the face nearest the pointer is **framed and named**, as the sky frames a
    body: the photo dims round it and four corners close in. The frame glides between faces
    (registered custom properties).
  - **Names:** pointing at a name in a day's "With" row frames that person in the photo.
  - **Lightbox:** clicking opens the lightbox, which now steps through an album of photos.
- **Names** link to LinkedIn. Pointed at, the mark takes LinkedIn's blue and pops, a ring
  ripples out once, a line draws in under the name, and the person's role rises over it.
- **The people** (`people.tsx`), a part at the end of the page, has three groups: the
  team, the other winners and the people at the summit. (Laid out side by side since the
  fourth round: see below.)
  - **The cards.** Each person is a card led by their face: a 4:5 portrait cut from the
    photos (`public/work/hackathon-wins/people/`, 20 WebP files in two sizes, 408 KB,
    EXIF stripped). Under it are their name, their role, where we met and their LinkedIn.
    The whole card is the link.
  - **Coming in.** The cards develop in one after another, as the prints do, when a group
    comes on screen.
  - **Pointed at.** A card lifts and lights in the award's colour, the face eases in, the
    site's corner ticks close round it, and the LinkedIn mark turns blue.
  - **Width.** The grid fills the width; on a phone the cards sit two to a row.
- **The home page's award chapter:**
  - It now shows an **album**: a stack of three prints (the hall, the three tracks, the
    summit wall).
  - The prints underneath peek out from under the top one. Pointed at, the stack fans out
    and holds still.
  - Every few seconds while it's on screen, the top print slides off to the right and is
    tucked under the others. Under reduced motion it stays still.
  - The stack can be stepped through under it, and it opens in the lightbox.
  - A third honour line, "**On stage**, Global AI & Digital Summit, Seoul · 21 Oct 2026",
    has an on-air light that flares once.
- The rail now has "The journey" and "The people". It ends where the journey opens out.
- **The glyph-orb look, everywhere** (`orb.ts`). It's after React Bits' Glyph Orb, as its
  docs describe it: a planet drawn in characters, with a shadow colour and a sunlit one,
  bands, a light angle and a glow. Its page couldn't be opened from the session that made
  this, so this follows the description, not the demo.
  - **A ramp.** Characters run from the faintest to the densest (`· : + * x # % @`), and a
    sphere's light is told by how dense each cell is. The Earth (in the hero, and on the
    journey) is drawn this way:
    - a soft terminator;
    - land denser and paler than the sea;
    - white clouds, and a glint where the sun reflects in the sea;
    - an atmosphere at the limb that brightens rather than thickens;
    - a night side that is dark but for city lights, or on the journey the faint shape of
      the land.
  - **A dark disc under every sphere** (Earth, the planets), soft at its rim, so the stars
    stop at its limb and it stands in front of the sky. There's no coloured ring at the
    edge.
  - **A glow over every canvas that draws a body:**
    - the home page's Earth and its layer of work;
    - each work page's body;
    - the bar's panel and its thumbnails;
    - the journey.

    It's a tight bloom and a wide one, gathered by halving the picture step by step, and
    multiplied by itself so that only highlights spill. A star already gives its own light,
    so its glow is kept low and it burns rather than blows out.

  - Planets keep their banded characters but lose the speckle outside their limb; the
    glow is their atmosphere.
  - The hero's orbits are particles now, small points of light, brighter and larger in
    front of Earth, instead of drawn lines.
- **The music switch** (`music.tsx`) is a pill that says what it is:
  - **Parts:** a note, four bars, and on a wide screen "Music on", "Music off" or "Play
    music".
  - **Playing:** it glows blue, the note sways, the bars move, and a note drifts up off it
    now and then.
  - **Off:** a stroke draws through the note and the bars lie flat.
  - **Before the browser allows sound:** a ring breathes round it, asking to be pressed.
  - **Reduced motion:** all of it holds still.
- Removed: the page's end photo, and the "Pictures from Seoul to come" note with its
  `Until`.

### Third round

- **The album opens at once.** The lightbox was loaded with `next/dynamic`, a lazy
  component, which React reveals through Suspense. Suspense holds back reveals for about
  300 ms, so the first click waited 485 ms for the view even with its code fetched. Now
  `lightbox-lazy.ts` fetches the lightbox once a picture is near and holds it; a click
  renders it at once. The home page's canvas also stops drawing while the lightbox is
  open.
- **The album's arrows** are round buttons on its left and right edges, on the stack and
  in the lightbox; the dots under it only show the place.
- **One width everywhere.** A token, `--edge` (6vw, or the gutter on a phone, in
  `spacing.css`), sets the side space on every page:
  - the hackathon page, its journey and its people;
  - every work page, with its pictures in a sticky second column on a wide screen;
  - the story, with its photograph beside the letter;
  - the work, resume and changelog lists, as grids across the width;
  - contact, the not-found page and the footer.

  Reading text keeps a measure of its own (about 46rem).

- **The content first on the journey.** The globe takes the narrower column now, its
  characters are drawn at 68% and its glow at 0.6. The text and prints get the wider
  column. The globe and its land map aren't drawn or fetched until the journey is within a
  screen.
- **No vertical lines.** The rule beside each day's heading, the rail's line, the work
  page's status line, the bar's divider, the viewer's borders and the drawer's edge are
  gone. The rail marks the current part with a dot, and the drawer uses a shadow.
- **The supernova** (`nova.tsx`) at the head of the hackathon page plays:
  - **Pointer:** its shell bulges away from the pointer, sparks of characters fly off it
    as it moves, and the core flares as it nears. The site's reticle follows it.
  - **Click:** sets it off again. A shockwave of characters runs out, sparks scatter, and
    the core flashes.
  - **Drag:** spins it, and it coasts to a stop.
  - **Touch and keyboard:** a tap sets it off and a swipe across spins it. Enter or Space
    sets it off.
  - **Reduced motion:** it holds still, and a press only flares the core.
  - **Cost:** it draws only while on screen, and more slowly when idle.
- **Speed.**
  - The home page's canvas bakes the glow into its layers when they redraw, rather than
    blurring every frame.
  - The small portraits draw at most every 44 ms, at 1.5× pixel density, and gather their
    glow every third draw.
  - The greeting's full stop now glows from a layer behind it rather than a text shadow.
    The shadow widened the text's painted box, so the browser took the dot, lit last, for
    the page's largest paint, at about 3.8 s.
  - The contact page's horizon has a soft rim of light in place of a sharp line.

### Fifth round

- **The supernova is part of the sky behind the head** (`nova.tsx`, `award.module.css`):
  - **Size:** about 60rem across (68% of the screen's width at most).
  - **Placement:** its heart sits up in the page's top left corner, clear of the words.
    Its shell runs out behind the index and off the edges of the screen; it never widens
    the page. On a phone its heart is in the top right corner instead.
  - **Faded:** it's drawn at 60%, and fainter the further it reaches, so where it passes
    behind the title it is only a texture. Its core and spikes are drawn at half their
    strength.
  - **Layering:** it's under everything on the page and takes no clicks. The back link
    and the index stay over it.
  - **Bursts:** it still goes off every ten seconds or so.
  - **The pointer:** it feels the pointer pass over it through the page (the bulge, the
    sparks, the core warming). A click on the empty sky round it, not on a word or a
    link, sets it off.
  - **Removed:** dragging to spin it, and the reticle round the pointer, since both would
    fight with the text over it.
  - **Cost:** it's drawn at the screen's own pixels at most, about fifteen times a second
    when nothing is happening. At rest it reuses its characters.

### Sixth round

- **The supernova stays in the sky** (`nova.tsx`, `award.tsx`, `award.module.css`).
  - **Fixed:** it's held in the screen's top left corner while the page scrolls over it,
    from the head to the people (on a phone, the top right).
  - **Layering:** it's over the stars and under everything on the page. It's mounted
    outside the article, whose arrival animation would otherwise carry it.
  - **Bursts:** every twenty seconds or so, here and on the home page's award chapter
    (`NOVA_EVERY`, `nova-burst.ts`). The first comes four seconds after the page arrives,
    once it has settled.
  - **Cost:** since it's always on screen, it no longer redraws at rest. Its thousands of
    characters are drawn onto a layer of their own once, and again only while the
    pointer pushes it or the screen changes size. A frame copies that layer, with the
    flash, the shockwave's own cells, the spikes and the sparks over it.
  - **At rest:** it breathes in CSS, its brightness rising and falling over nine seconds,
    which costs the page nothing. It rests while the zoom view is open. The slow turn is
    gone.
- **A larger globe, a wider column for the days** (`journey.module.css`).
  - **The days:** they take about 72% of the content's width (0.55fr against 1.45fr).
  - **The globe:** larger than its column. It reaches back behind the index to the
    screen's left edge, a world rising out of the left of the sky, and on a little way
    under the days, which are drawn over it.
  - **Its size:** at 1440 px its canvas is 706 px wide, against 384 before, so the Earth
    is about 1.8 times the size. It's drawn a little more quietly (`QUIET` 0.6), since
    there's more of it.
  - **The index:** it stays over the globe and the supernova on a soft pool of shadow,
    its words with a dark halo, so it reads over either and stays clickable.
  - **On a phone:** the globe keeps its band under the index.
