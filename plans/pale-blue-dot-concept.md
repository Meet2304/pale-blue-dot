# The Pale Blue Dot — Concept & Vision

## The name
"The Pale Blue Dot" — after the Voyager photograph of Earth as a single point
of light from ~6 billion km away. Chosen because it holds two things at once
without resolving them: everything that's ever mattered happened on
something that small, and I don't yet know what I'll add to it — but I
intend to add something before I'm gone.

## The throughline (who this site is actually about)
Across the work that's already real — leading Serin end-to-end rather than
staying in one lane, taking on Project Phoenix without being assigned it,
turning down a ready-made path (Astar) for one I'd have to build myself —
the same move keeps happening: choosing to own the whole thing instead of
taking the piece that's given. That's the thesis the rest of the site is
built to demonstrate, not state.

Underneath that: I don't know my purpose yet, but I want to leave something
behind. This site is that attempt, one piece at a time.

## Design principle
Majesty through restraint, not accumulation. One dominant gesture, executed
with total control, beats several good ones stacked together. Every element
has to earn its place — if removing it loses nothing real, cut it. The
feeling the site gives is part of the message: care in the execution is
supposed to be evidence of the same care applied to everything else.

Evidence over claims throughout: show the decision and the trade-off inside
each piece of work, don't just list outcomes. Nothing gets explained in the
UI that can instead be felt — except the Note (see below), which is the one
deliberate exception.

## Site structure (as conceived so far)

1. **Hero** — built. See below.
2. **The Note** — not yet built. The one place on the site that explains the
   vision directly, in plain language, rather than implying it.
3. **Navigation** — not yet built. Needs to be simple and easy to use;
   specifics still undecided.
4. **Project showcase** — not yet built. Story-driven, not a list. See
   principles below.
5. **Changelog** (life-inflection-points page) — concept only, not yet
   built. See below.
6. **Blog / writing** — deliberately deferred. Do not build until there's
   real intent to sustain it — an empty or abandoned one is worse than none.

## Hero section (current build)

- Night sea horizon: glowing blue gradient with subtle noise texture,
  reflected in the water below. Starry sky above, twinkling.
- Water reacts to mouse movement (interactive displacement).
- Load sequence: black → single point of light ignites low-center → grows
  into the horizon → stars and noise resolve → water fades in and goes
  interactive. Full spec for this sequence lives in `hero-section-spec.md`.
- Scroll triggers a warp animation into the night sky toward a star, which
  leads into the next section. **Stars = individual project entries** —
  the warp isn't a generic transition, it's navigation with meaning: each
  star is one of the project stories.
- **Hero line:** "What's missing, I make."
- **Hint line** (smaller, quieter, positioned in the space above "SCROLL"):
  "so small, so much of it matters." — a deliberate hint, not a summary.
  Its job is to make someone curious enough to find the Note, not to explain
  anything on its own.

## The Note (vision statement — for the dedicated page)

Two drafted versions below. Leaning toward the short version for something
meant to be read in one breath — the long version earns its length if the
photograph itself should register as a real image before pivoting to me.

### Short version
> Seen from far enough away, this whole planet is a single point of light.
> Everything that's ever mattered happened on it.
>
> I don't know my purpose yet. I'm not pretending otherwise here. But I want
> to leave something behind before I'm done — some small addition to that
> point of light.
>
> Everything on this site is part of that attempt.

### Long version
> There's a photograph of Earth taken from about six billion kilometers
> away. In it, this whole planet is a single point of light — barely there,
> easy to miss. Everything anyone has ever done, ever loved, ever built,
> happened on that point.
>
> I think about that a lot.
>
> I don't know yet what I'm here to do. I haven't landed on a purpose I can
> state with any confidence, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise on this
> page. But I know I want to leave something behind — some small addition to
> that point of light that outlasts me.
>
> Everything I build is part of that attempt. Not because I've figured it
> out, but because waiting until I have felt like the wrong way to spend the
> time.
>
> This site is where that attempt lives, one piece at a time.

## Project showcase — principles (not yet built)

Each entry gets the same shape, so it stays disciplined instead of
sprawling into a portfolio:
1. What the situation was before I touched it
2. What I specifically chose to take on — my decision, not the team's output
3. The trade-off or moment I could've stayed in my lane and didn't
4. What that changed about how I work now (name the actual shift, don't
   just claim growth happened)

Two or three entries at launch (Serin, Project Phoenix, maybe Linea), each a
tight paragraph or two — not a case study, not a feature list. Landing spot
after the warp animation should be the strongest ownership arc first
(currently: Serin).

## Changelog — concept (not yet built)

Not a full life log from birth — that fights the restraint principle by
design (it never has to decide what's worth including). Unit is the
**inflection point**: a handful of moments that actually changed the
trajectory or explain why decisions get made a certain way now (e.g.
"decided not to go to Astar" — a decision, not a resume line).

Test for whether something earns an entry: *does this change how a stranger
understands why I make the choices I make, or is it just a thing that
happened?* If the latter, it's a diary entry, not a changelog entry.

Framing: versioning myself instead of narrating myself — terse, dated, one
line per entry. Possible future tie-in: connecting changelog entries to the
star field (unresolved, worth revisiting once the project showcase exists).
